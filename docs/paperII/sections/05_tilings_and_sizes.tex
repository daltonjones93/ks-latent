%======================================================================
\section{Shifted tilings and one model from \texorpdfstring{$44^2$ to $440^2$}{44x44 to 440x440}}
\label{sec:da_results}
%======================================================================

This section asks whether the local structure that lets the model transfer
also lets a filter work, with one model and one setting, on domains of any
size. The filter runs entirely in the latent space of the learned model and
observes the physical field. It never uses the equations of the system, and
its model never sees a clean field.

%----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Setting}
\label{sec:da_setting}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

\para{The model.} Every 2D experiment uses the noisy-trained 12-channel
model of Sec.~\ref{sec:model_validation}, with the same weights at every
domain size.

\para{Twin experiments.} At each domain size the truth is a trajectory of
the PDE, integrated at the deployment resolution from a spun-up state.
Every $0.5$ time units (5 model steps, about $0.08$ Lyapunov times) the
field is observed at the points of a regular network with spacing
$2.75$, half the patch length, so four observations per site, with
independent Gaussian errors of standard deviation $0.1$ in standardized
units ($0.62$ in field units). The networks are listed in
Table~\ref{tab:da_networks}. Each tiling's initial ensemble is its
encoding of the initial truth plus independent perturbations of
standard deviation $0.1$ per latent coordinate. Each run has $100$
cycles, and all reported values are steady-state averages over cycles
$51$--$100$.

\para{The filter.} The per-site local ensemble transform filter of
Sec.~\ref{sec:da_method}, Gaspari--Cohn half-width $1.5$ sites, RTPS
$\alpha=0.7$, model noise $0.02$ per coordinate and step, $64$ members per
tiling and four shifted tilings blended by Eq.~\eqref{eq:blend}. These
values are the same in every 2D experiment of this paper; none was tuned
per domain size, per seed or per experiment.

\para{What is measured.} The \emph{error} is the root-mean-square
difference between the analysis mean, decoded and blended, and the true
field, over all grid points, in field units. We compare it with what can
be had without a dynamical model: cubic-spline interpolation of the same
observations ($0.81$--$0.83$ at every size) and climatological optimal
interpolation ($0.80$ at $44^2$); with the decoder's own error on the
encoded truth, $\|\Dec(\E(u))-u\|$, single and blended (the \emph{decoder
floor}); and with a filter that knows the equations, the physical-space
local ensemble transform filter of Sec.~\ref{sec:comparator} run with the
true PDE ($0.076$ at its best localization). The run without assimilation
has errors of $4.2$--$5.3$ ($10.1$ for one truth at $330^2$, Table~\ref{tab:da_2d_sizes}). \emph{Calibration} is the ratio of ensemble
spread to error, $1$ for an ensemble that represents its own
uncertainty. Free-run skill, the ratio of the free-run error to the
analysis error, is reported in Table~\ref{tab:da_2d_sizes} for comparison
with earlier latent filters.

%----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Shifted tilings}
\label{sec:da_tilings}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

With one tiling the analysis error at $44^2$ is $0.457$ (seed 0; $0.431$ and
$0.418$ for seeds 1 and 2). With four shifted tilings it is $0.324$, $0.310$
and $0.302$: about $30\%$ lower on every seed, from the same model with no
retraining (Fig.~\ref{fig:da_2d}f and Table~\ref{tab:da_2d_ensemble}). Two
tilings, offset diagonally, give $0.369$, and sixteen, offset by a quarter
patch, $0.287$. The calibration is $0.86$ with one tiling, $0.92$--$0.99$ with
four and $0.51$ with sixteen: the independent spreads average down faster
than the errors once the tilings overlap strongly, so four is the
calibrated choice and sixteen the accurate one. Uniform weights instead
of Hann weights give $0.313$ at calibration $0.63$.

\para{It is the offsets, not the members.} Four ensembles of 64 members on
the \emph{same} tiling, blended the same way, give $0.450$, $0.423$ and $0.412$,
no better than one tiling, and a calibration of $0.44$--$0.48$
(Table~\ref{tab:da_2d_ensemble}(iii)). Splitting a fixed total of 64
members into four tilings of 16 gives $0.352$, $0.334$ and $0.318$, against
$0.457$, $0.431$ and $0.418$ for one ensemble of 64: at equal cost the tilings
are $22$--$24\%$ more accurate on every seed. Shifted tilings are a better use
of members than a larger ensemble.

\para{Why it works.} With one tiling the error is tied to the patch
lattice. It is largest at patch edges, $0.49$--$0.58$ against $0.38$ at patch
centres (Fig.~\ref{fig:tilings}c), and the mean gradient across a patch
edge is $1.73$ times that inside a patch, against $1.02$ in the truth
(Fig.~\ref{fig:tilings}f). The lattice-tied part is not limited to
the seams. Decomposed by wavenumber, the errors of the four tilings are
only weakly correlated: the mean correlation between tilings is $0.37$--$0.42$
at wavenumbers below $1.6$ and $0.11$ above, at both $44^2$ and $220^2$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:tilings}e). Averaging weakly correlated errors removes most
of them: the blend reduces the error variance by a factor of $1.4$--$1.9$ at
wavenumbers below $1.6$ and $3.9$ above (Fig.~\ref{fig:tilings}d). The seam
ratio of the blend is $1.02$, the truth's. Feeding the blended analysis
back into each tiling's encoder makes things worse, because the blended
field is not the decoding of any latent state; the tilings therefore
cycle independently and are combined only for output.

\para{Exactness and cost.} Each tiling's filter is exactly local
(Proposition~\ref{prop:blockdiag}), and the blend is a fixed linear map
of their outputs, so the composite analysis is exactly local too. The
four filters are independent and run in parallel; run in one process,
four tilings cost $2.5$--$2.9$ times one at every size, because the truth
integration and the observation draws are shared (Sec.~\ref{sec:da_cost}).

\subsection{Accuracy and calibration at the native size}
\label{sec:da_accuracy}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

At $44^2$ the analysis error is $0.30$--$0.32$ over three seeds, against $0.81$--$0.83$
for interpolating the same observations and $0.80$ for climatological
optimal interpolation: the learned dynamics carry information between
observation times and between observation points that the observations
alone do not. At the observed points the error is $0.31$--$0.33$, half the
observation error, so the analysis is not simply drawing towards the
data. The prior, one cycle earlier, has an error of $0.34$--$0.39$; the
latent model's own error over one cycle, measured on an independent
truth, is small, so the filter's error is a persistent part that the
analysis does not remove rather than error the model regrows each cycle.
The ensemble is well calibrated, with spread over error $0.92$--$0.99$.
RTPS weights of $0.5$, $0.7$ and $0.9$ give $0.322$, $0.324$ and $0.340$ at
calibrations $0.78$, $0.92$ and $1.16$; we use $0.7$, the calibrated choice
within $1\%$ of the best error.

\para{The decoder is not the limit.} The decoder floor is $0.24$--$0.25$ for one
tiling, about half of that tiling's analysis error, and $0.13$--$0.14$ for the
blend, well below the blended analysis's $0.30$--$0.33$
(Table~\ref{tab:da_2d_sizes}). Most of the remaining error is therefore
the filter's, not the reconstruction's.

\para{The analysis belongs in latent space.} One could use the learned
model only as a forecast device and do the analysis in physical space.
We tested this with the same model, encoding each member on the four
tilings, propagating, decoding and blending, and analysing the blended
members with the physical-space local ensemble transform filter of
Sec.~\ref{sec:comparator}, with the localization radius and RTPS tuned on the
clean-trained model.
Its best error is $0.354$, $0.345$ and $0.338$ for seeds 0--2, at a calibration of
$1.22$--$1.27$, against $0.324$, $0.310$ and $0.302$ at $0.92$--$0.99$ for the latent
analysis; with one tiling the two are equal ($0.461$ and $0.457$). The latent
analysis is $8$--$11\%$ more accurate on every seed and better calibrated.
A physical-space analysis must re-encode fields that the decoder never
produced, and the latent analysis does not.

\para{Gaussianity.} The latent coordinates are close to Gaussian in
climatology (mean absolute skewness per coordinate $0.20$, excess kurtosis
$-0.07$), and 16-member one-cycle priors show mean absolute skewness of $0.38$ in
latent space and $0.40$ in physical space, within the sampling noise of about $0.6$
for 16 members. At this cycle length the latent space offers neither an
advantage nor a disadvantage for a Gaussian filter.

\subsection{One model and one setting from \texorpdfstring{$44^2$ to $440^2$}{44x44 to 440x440}}
\label{sec:da_2d}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

Table~\ref{tab:da_2d_sizes} and Fig.~\ref{fig:da_2d} give the result
that the locality was built for. The same model and the same filter
settings are deployed on square domains from $44^2$ to $440^2$, a factor of
100 in area and in latent dimension ($\dlat = 768$ to $76\,800$ per tiling).
The analysis error is $0.324$, $0.304$, $0.312$, $0.326$ and $0.311$ at $44^2$, $88^2$,
$220^2$, $330^2$ and $440^2$, with second seeds at $220^2$ and $330^2$ within $0.01$, and
the calibration stays between $0.92$ and $0.98$. With one tiling the error
is $0.42$--$0.46$ at every size; interpolation stays at $0.81$--$0.83$ and the
blended decoder floor at $0.13$--$0.14$. Nothing degrades with the domain,
and there is no sign of a size beyond which the method stops working
(Fig.~\ref{fig:da_2d}b,d). Figure~\ref{fig:da_snapshot} shows one analysis at $440^2$: the
blend follows the truth's structure at every scale the observations
resolve, and the patch-edge seams of a single tiling, visible in its error,
are absent from the blend's.

\para{The ensemble does not grow with the domain.} With four tilings, the
error is $0.450$, $0.352$, $0.330$, $0.324$ and $0.322$ for $8$, $16$, $32$, $64$ and $128$
members per tiling at $44^2$, and $0.329$, $0.315$, $0.312$ and $0.311$ for $16$ to $128$ at
$220^2$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:da_2d}c): accuracy saturates by about 32 members per
tiling at both sizes. Equation~\eqref{eq:nens_local} with the 2D KS
density of $0.043$ per unit area and the taper's support (radius $16.5$
length units) gives about $37$, consistent with that saturation.

\para{Without localization.} The same filter with the taper set to one
everywhere is the global ETKF in the latent space. Its error grows with
the domain at a fixed ensemble, and it stays bounded only while the
ensemble is larger than the number of unstable directions of the
attractor, $42$--$44$ at $44^2$, $93$--$101$ at $66^2$ and, extrapolating that density, about $170$ at $88^2$. At $44^2$,
with 64 and 128 members, it reaches $0.51$ and $0.37$ with four tilings
($0.69$ and $0.50$ with one), against $0.32$ localized. At $66^2$, 64
members fail ($2.26$ with four tilings, $3.22$ with one, against
interpolation's $0.82$), while 128 members give $0.49$ and $0.67$. At
$88^2$ both fail: $3.40$ and $1.76$ with four tilings, $4.07$ and $2.59$ with
one. The tilings reduce the error in every case but do not rescue a filter
whose ensemble is too small for the attractor. The localized filter,
with 64 members per tiling, gives $0.32$, $0.31$ and $0.30$ at the same three
sizes.

\para{Long runs.} Over 300 cycles at $220^2$ the error averages $0.31$ in each of cycles 51--100,
101--200 and 201--300, at a calibration of $0.97$ (Table~\ref{tab:da_robust}): the filter does not drift.
