\subsection{Ensemble filters, localization and extensive chaos}
\label{sec:bg_da}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

Ensemble Kalman filters
\citep{burgers1998,houtekamermitchell1998,whitakerhamill2002} estimate
forecast covariances from a small ensemble, and localization makes them
work when the ensemble is much smaller than the state: by tapering the
covariance \citep{houtekamermitchell2001,gaspari1999}, by analysing each
grid point from nearby observations
\citep{ott2004,hunt2007}, or by correcting sampling error without a
distance \citep{anderson2012}. The local ensemble transform Kalman filter
(LETKF) of \citet{hunt2007}, the form used here, computes one analysis per
grid point or region from the observations within a cut-off radius,
inflating the error variance of each observation with its distance
(observation-space or R-localization), and applies a deterministic
transform to the ensemble perturbations. The two common localizations are
related but not equivalent \citep{sakovbertino2011,greybush2011}. The case
for localization rests on local low dimensionality \citep{patil2001}: the
forecast errors within a region span few directions. Without
localization, a filter needs at least as many members as there are
unstable and neutral directions
\citep{trevisan2004,gurumoorthy2017,bocquetcarrassi2017}; for a
deterministic filter on Lorenz-96 the minimum is $n_+ + 1$, with $n_+$ the
number of positive Lyapunov exponents \citep{takeda2026}. Particle flow
filters offer a nonlinear alternative \citep{huvanleeuwen2021}.

In spatially extended chaos this requirement grows with the domain. The
Kaplan--Yorke dimension $\DKY$ \citep{kaplanyorke1979} and the number of
positive exponents of an extensively chaotic system grow in proportion to
its volume \citep{ruelle1982,manneville1985,egolfgreenside1994}, with a
density $\rho$ that does not depend on the domain; for the
Kuramoto--Sivashinsky (KS) equation in one dimension $\rho\approx0.22$ per
unit length \citep{edson2019}. An unlocalized filter must therefore grow
its ensemble with the domain, while a localized one needs only enough
members for the unstable directions inside its local region
(Sec.~\ref{sec:nens}).

\subsection{Data assimilation in learned latent spaces}
\label{sec:bg_latent}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

Learned surrogates of spatiotemporal chaos usually encode the whole field
into one latent vector and learn its evolution
\citep{linotgraham2020,linotgraham2022,racca2023}, and several reproduce
the Lyapunov spectrum of the system at the size they were trained on
\citep{pathak2017,vlachas2020,ozalpmagri2025}. Filters in such latent
spaces have been built with global latents and no localization
\citep{peyron2021,chen2023roadenkf,pasmans2025,tong2026laeenkf}. The
reduced-order autodifferentiable ensemble Kalman filter of
\citet{chen2023roadenkf}, which includes KS among its tests, explicitly
does not pursue covariance localization, and the latent autoencoder
filter of \citet{tong2026laeenkf} lists it as future work. The reason is
structural: a global latent vector has no notion of distance, so there is
nothing to taper. Latent score-based filters note that encoding the state
into a latent vector may lose the local structure that LETKF-type methods
exploit \citep{si2025latentensf}. Learned corrections to the ensemble Kalman
gain, with a learned distance-based localization, can outperform a tuned
LETKF on KS at the size they were trained on \citep{bach2025}.

\citet{jones2026transfer}, hereafter Paper~I, take the opposite route: a
latent \emph{field} on a lattice of sites, built from weight-shared
operators of finite reach, so that one trained model runs on domains of any
size and reproduces the true Lyapunov spectra, $\DKY$ and their growth with
the domain in 1D and 2D KS and in Kolmogorov flow. Such a latent has a
geometry, and the question this paper asks is what that geometry is worth
to a filter.

\subsection{Imperfect models and their remedies}
\label{sec:bg_model_error}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

A learned model is worth using where a physical one is wrong. A line of
work learns the forecast model itself from sparse, noisy observations, by
alternating ensemble data assimilation and network training
\citep{brajard2020,bocquet2020}. There, as here, the comparison that
matters is with data assimilation run with a physical model:
\citet{brajard2020} report that on the 40-variable Lorenz-96 system their
learned convolutional surrogate, trained and used at that one size,
recovers the positive Lyapunov exponents, and that an ensemble filter
with it has an analysis error $2.4$ times that of the same filter with the
true model, and well below interpolation. With an imperfect physical model
the standard remedies are inflation and relaxation to prior spread
\citep{whitakerhamill2012}, online estimation of model parameters by state
augmentation \citep{anderson2001,ruiz2013}, and bias correction with
additive model error \citep{dee2005}. A fair comparison gives the
physics-based filter all of them (Sec.~\ref{sec:da_model_error}).

Finally, the shifted tilings of Sec.~\ref{sec:tilings_method} are cycle
spinning \citep{coifmandonoho1995} on the patch lattice: averaging
estimates computed on shifted copies of a grid removes the artefacts tied
to the grid.

\subsection{What is new here}
\label{sec:bg_novelty}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

That localization makes ensemble filters work with small ensembles is
established, and so is data assimilation in a learned latent space.
Table~\ref{tab:prior_work} summarizes the closest work. What we add is
their combination, made exact: a latent observation operator that is
exactly local, so that a per-site ensemble filter in latent space needs no
truncation and keeps working at any domain size with one model and one
setting; shifted tilings that remove the errors tied to the patch lattice;
and a comparison with physics-based filters whose models are wrong in ways
that their standard remedies can and cannot correct.
