\section{The one-dimensional case}
\label{sec:da_1d}
%======================================================================

Two results from 1D KS complete the picture (Fig.~\ref{fig:da_1d};
details, the table and the observation-density sweep in
Appendix~\ref{app:da_1d}). They use the 1D model of Paper~I (Sec.~\ref{sec:ks1d}), deployed at
$L=200$ and $400$ without retraining, a single tiling, and the particle flow
filter or the stochastic ensemble Kalman filter at a fixed observation
spacing of $3.125$; skill is the ratio of free-run to analysis error.

\para{The ensemble an unlocalized filter needs doubles with the domain.}
With localization, every ensemble of 16 or more members gives a skill of
$2.3$--$3.0$ at both sizes and with both filters. Without it, the filter is
worse than no assimilation until the ensemble is large enough, and how
large depends on the domain: at $L=200$, where the truth has $n_+=26$--$27$, it is
marginal with 64 members and works with 128; at $L=400$, where $n_+=51$, it
fails with 64, is marginal with 128 and works only with 256. The two
filters differ by at most $0.4$ in skill. This is Eq.~\eqref{eq:nens_local}
and its unlocalized counterpart, measured.

\para{A distance beats a distance-free correction.} Sampling error
correction \citep{anderson2012} localizes without a distance and is the
natural alternative for a latent space that has none. On one latent at
$L=200$, in the forms in which it runs (the corrected matrix projected onto
the positive semidefinite cone, or the serial correction of each
regression coefficient that \citet{anderson2012} uses), Gaspari--Cohn
localization in the latent field beats it at every ensemble size below
256, by a factor of up to $1.7$ against the projected form and up to $3.5$
against the serial one, which does not help at all below 128 members.
SEC removes sampling noise from correlations; a distance taper also
removes the many weak true correlations between distant sites, which a
small ensemble cannot afford to estimate. Giving the latent a geometry is
what makes this possible.
