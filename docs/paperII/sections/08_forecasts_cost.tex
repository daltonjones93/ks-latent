%======================================================================
\section{Forecasts, robustness, observation density and cost}
\label{sec:da_robust}
%======================================================================

\para{Forecasts from the analyses.} A better analysis should give a better
forecast, not only a smoother picture. From steady-state analyses at
$44^2$ we forecast each tiling's analysis mean with the learned model,
without noise, and blend the forecasts (Fig.~\ref{fig:forecasts},
Table~\ref{tab:da_forecast}). The blended forecast's error is $26\%$ below
that of the single-tiling forecast after one cycle and $15$--$17\%$ below it
at $0.4$--$1.7$ Lyapunov times. It reaches half the field's standard
deviation after $1.26$ Lyapunov times (seeds 0--2: $1.16$--$1.40$), against $1.10$ for
the single-tiling analysis, $1.62$ for a forecast started from the encoded
true state and $0.33$ for persistence, and the field's standard deviation
after $2.39$, against $2.06$, $2.70$ and $0.74$. The tilings recover a third to a half
of the forecast skill that separates the single-tiling analysis from a
perfect initial condition for this model. Beyond about three Lyapunov
times all forecasts are decorrelated from the truth; the blend's error
then stays lower only because it averages four partly independent
forecasts.

\para{Robustness.} Started from an ensemble drawn from an independent
climatology, with no knowledge of the truth, the filter's error falls
from $1.33$ to within $10\%$ of its steady value in 8 cycles, and the analysis
then converges onto the one started from the encoded truth: the
difference between the two decays by a factor of about ten every ten
cycles, to $10^{-8}$ by cycle 100 (Table~\ref{tab:da_robust}). With the
observation error misspecified by a factor of two in either direction
the error rises by $4$--$8\%$ ($0.351$ and $0.338$ against $0.324$), and the calibration
moves the way it should ($0.58$ and $1.40$). A 300-cycle run at $220^2$ holds $0.31$ throughout.

\para{Observation density.} Table~\ref{tab:da_robust} and Fig.~\ref{fig:da_2d}
use observations every $2.75$ length units, four per site. With twice as
many in each direction (spacing $1.4$) the error is $0.226$ with four tilings and
$0.333$ with one, against $0.539$ for interpolation; with half as many (spacing
$5.5$, one observation per site) it is $0.769$ and $0.990$, against $3.01$ for
interpolation, a factor of four. At this sparse spacing the filter stays
bounded over 300 cycles (means $0.77$, $0.77$ and $0.79$ over cycles 51--100,
101--200 and 201--300), but its error relative to interpolation is no
longer the whole story: the learned model must carry the state between
sparse observations, and on longer runs with sparser networks the
stability of the learned propagator becomes the limit. Failsafes for that
regime, a propagator trained with latent noise, a gate that falls back to
it off the attractor, and the reset of a tiling that departs from the
others, are the subject of a follow-up study.

\subsection{Cost and memory}
\label{sec:da_cost}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

Table~\ref{tab:da_cost} gives what the filter costs, measured one run at
a time on an otherwise idle workstation (three threads, 64 members per
tiling, 100 cycles). The times are for the whole run, including the
integration of the PDE truth and the observation draws. One tiling takes
$9$~s at $44^2$ and $1266$~s at $440^2$, four tilings $26$~s and $3542$~s, or $35$~s per
cycle at $\dlat=76\,800$ per tiling. Over the full range, a factor of 100 in
area, the time grows almost in proportion to the area (least-squares
slope $1.05$ on log axes for both one and four tilings); above $220^2$ it
grows somewhat faster (slope $1.3$), although the work per site does not
depend on the domain; we have not identified the cause (memory traffic is
the likely one). Four tilings in one process cost $2.5$--$2.9$ times
one, not four, because the truth and the observations are shared; the
tilings are independent and could run in parallel, which we did not time. Peak
memory, $0.7$~GB at $44^2$ and $14.6$ and $17.2$~GB at $440^2$ for one and four
tilings, grows more slowly than the area (slope $0.68$) and includes the
stored truth trajectory, $6.6$~GB at $440^2$. The per-site filter never forms a
$\dlat\times\dlat$ matrix, which at $440^2$ would take $47$~GB in double precision.
We have not profiled the per-site loop, which runs in Python and is the
obvious target for a vectorized implementation. The observation Jacobian
itself is cheap: its twelve forward passes take $2.4$~ms at $44^2$ and $119$~ms at
$440^2$, where dense reverse mode would need one pass per observation
(Appendix~\ref{app:da_jacobian}).
