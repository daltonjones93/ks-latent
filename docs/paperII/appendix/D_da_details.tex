\subsection{Cost of the observation Jacobian}
\label{app:da_jacobian}

Figure~\ref{fig:jacobian_timing} times the latent observation Jacobian at
every deployment size, with the observation network of the runs. The
coloured construction, $c=12$ forward-mode passes through the decoder,
gives the same matrix as dense reverse-mode differentiation to single
precision (largest difference $2\times10^{-7}$ up to $132^2$), and its time grows
slightly more slowly than the latent dimension: $2.4$~ms at $44^2$ and
$119$~ms at $440^2$. Reverse mode needs one pass per observation and grows
almost with the square of the dimension, $32$ times slower at $132^2$; we did
not run it beyond that size.

\subsection{One-dimensional experiments}
\label{app:da_1d}

Table~\ref{tab:da_1d} gives the 1D results summarized in Sec.~\ref{sec:da_1d}. They use the 1D
model of Paper~I, a single tiling, and either the particle flow filter or the stochastic ensemble
Kalman filter with Gaspari--Cohn localization of half-width 3 sites, without RTPS.

\para{Observation density.} With the particle flow filter at $L=200$ the skill is $5.2$, $2.9$, $1.2$ and
$1.0$ at observation spacings $1.6$, $3.1$, $6.25$ and $12.5$: with a single tiling, assimilation fails once
the spacing reaches the site length ($6.25$), well below the pattern wavelength $2\sqrt2\,\pi\approx8.9$.
An earlier version of the 1D experiment scaled the observation stride with the domain, which doubled
the spacing with every doubling of $L$; at $L=200$ its localized skill was about $1.3$, against $2.9$ at the
fixed spacing used here.

\para{Sampling error correction.} At the training size $L=100$, in an earlier comparison with 40
cycles, SEC applied to a global latent vector beat Gaspari--Cohn applied to the local field at every
ensemble size, with errors $3$--$19\%$ lower. That comparison confounds the localization with the
architecture (two different autoencoders), and at $L=100$ the taper is non-zero on 11 of the 16 sites
of the ring, so it hardly localizes. At $L=200$ the comparison is made on one latent. The
SEC-corrected covariance formed elementwise is indefinite at five of six ensemble sizes, and the
particle flow filter, which factorizes it, fails; this is a property of the matrix form, not of SEC,
and the projected and serial forms of Appendix~\ref{app:da_filters} run at every size.

\subsection{Noisy training: the round-trip test}
\label{app:da_roundtrip}

Forty true states of an independent 2D KS trajectory at $44^2$, standardized with each model's training
statistics, are perturbed and passed through the encoder and decoder; the ratio of the error after the
round trip to the error before it is below one when the round trip moves the field towards the truth.
For white grid-point noise of standard deviation $0.05$, $0.1$, $0.2$ and $0.4$ the ratio is $0.95$, $0.53$, $0.35$
and $0.29$ for the clean-trained model and $0.81$, $0.44$, $0.29$ and $0.23$ for the noisy-trained one; for
smooth noise (correlation length 4 grid points) it is $1.35$, $1.09$, $1.02$ and $0.99$ against $1.26$, $1.07$,
$1.01$ and $0.99$; for latent-space noise of standard deviation $0.05$--$0.2$ it is $1.00$--$1.05$ for both. The
reconstruction error of unperturbed states is $0.046$ and $0.039$ (\texttt{exp\_j18b\_roundtrip.py}).

\subsection{Run-to-run spread and transients}
\label{app:da_tuning}

Seeds change the initial ensemble, the observation noise and the model noise; seeds 1 and 2 also
change the truth's initial state. At $44^2$ the three seeds give $0.302$--$0.324$, and second seeds at $220^2$
and $330^2$ are within $0.01$ of the first (Table~\ref{tab:da_2d_sizes}); differences below about $0.02$ are
within run-to-run variation. Started from the encoded truth, the analysis error rises over the first
cycles as the ensemble spreads, and is first within $10\%$ of its steady value by cycle 4 at every size.
Its 10-cycle means over cycles 51--100 vary by up to $\pm6\%$ at $44^2$, $\pm2\%$ at $88^2$ and $\pm1\%$ or less at $220^2$ and
beyond, the smaller domains averaging over fewer independent regions. Long runs with sparse
observations are not bitwise reproducible from run to run (multithreaded floating-point reductions,
amplified by chaos), so differences between methods smaller than the seed spread are not
significant there. The per-cycle values of every run are stored with its summary.
