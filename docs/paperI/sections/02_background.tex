\subsection{Extensive chaos and its Lyapunov analysis}
\label{sec:bg_extensive}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

A spatially extended system is extensively chaotic when the dimension of
its attractor grows in proportion to its volume. Ruelle conjectured that
the Lyapunov spectrum of turbulence has a large-volume limit, a density of
exponents per unit volume \citep{ruelle1982}; numerical studies of
coupled map lattices established such a limit for lattice maps
\citep{livi1986,isola1990}, and \citet{bunimovichsinai1988} proved
related results for weakly coupled maps. For the Kuramoto--Sivashinsky
equation extensivity of the Kaplan--Yorke dimension $\DKY$
\citep{kaplanyorke1979} was found by \citet{manneville1985}, and
\citet{edson2019} give $\DKY\approx0.226L-0.16$ for $L\gtrsim80$.
\citet{egolfgreenside1994} showed that extensivity holds in a
representative pattern-forming system but that the dimension density is
not set by the two-point correlation length, so the length that controls
it must be found by other means. Not every degree of freedom counted by
$\DKY$ is local: \citet{yang2009} separate a finite set of ``physical''
Lyapunov modes from the rest in KS, and \citet{takeuchi2009} show that
large chaotic systems can carry collective modes. Spectra are computed
with the method of \citet{benettin1980}.

Two results are especially close to our question. The first is that a
lattice spectrum computed on a small subsystem, suitably rescaled,
predicts the spectrum, $\DKY$ and entropy of the large system
\citep{carretero1999}. A trained local model is a learned lattice map, so
this is the dynamical-systems basis for expecting that a model trained
on a small domain can reproduce the dimension of a large one. The second
is that perturbations in spatiotemporal chaos spread at a finite speed,
measured by comoving (velocity-dependent) Lyapunov exponents
\citep{kaneko1986,deisslerkaneko1987}. We measure this speed for 1D KS
but find that it does not set the reach a model needs
(Sec.~\ref{sec:receptive}).

In two-dimensional flows without large-scale dissipation the inverse
cascade makes the statistics, and the dimension, depend on the domain
size; linear drag arrests it (Sec.~\ref{sec:kolmogorov}). The most closely
related dimension study of Kolmogorov flow, \citet{kolmogorovregimes2026},
computes $\DKY$ across Reynolds numbers and forcing wavenumbers in a fixed
box without drag.

\subsection{Learned surrogates of spatiotemporal chaos}
\label{sec:bg_surrogates}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

\para{Global latent models and their Lyapunov validation.} The common
design encodes the whole field into one latent vector and learns its
evolution, for example with autoencoders and neural ordinary differential
equations on an inertial manifold \citep{linotgraham2020,linotgraham2022}
or with convolutional autoencoders and echo state networks
\citep{racca2023}. Learned models of KS reproduce its Lyapunov spectrum: a
reservoir computer at $L=60$ \citep{pathak2017}, reservoir and recurrent
networks \citep{vlachas2020}, and latent models with their covariant
Lyapunov vectors \citep{ozalp2023,ozalpmagri2025}; a convolutional
surrogate of the 40-variable Lorenz-96 system learned from sparse, noisy
observations recovers its positive exponents \citep{brajard2020,bocquet2020}.
Each validation is at the single domain size the model was trained on.

\para{Local models and larger domains.} Parallel reservoir computers
forecast KS on large domains with one reservoir per subdomain
\citep{pathak2018}; the reservoirs can share weights, but a new set is
trained for each domain size, and the exponents reported are the PDE's,
not the model's. Patch-decomposed autoencoders with neural ODEs model KS
and Kolmogorov flow with low-dimensional latents per patch
\citep{constanteamores2024}, again one model per size, validated by
short-time tracking and flow statistics; in a minimal flow unit, which is
non-extensive by construction, a single global latent suffices
\citep{constanteamores2025}. Translation-equivariant models can be run on
larger domains than they were trained on: learned discretizations
\citep{barsinai2019}, learned corrections to a coarse Navier--Stokes
solver on a Kolmogorov domain twice as large \citep{kochkov2021}, learned
coarse simulators of KS \citep{stachenfeld2022}, local neural operators on
unseen geometries \citep{li2024lno}, and transformer operators with a
locality bias on domains up to ten times larger per axis
\citep{devilleroche2026}; fully convolutional networks trained on image
crops are the computer-vision form of the idea \citep{long2015}, and
generalization to finer resolution is a different question
\citep{sakarvadia2026}. These studies validate by short-time error or by
statistics, not by the dimension of the attractor. Local neural-field
latents have been reported to fail on KS, with the decoder as the
bottleneck \citep{aroma2024}.

\para{Limited-area models and rollout training.} Our window training is a
learned limited-area model: the interior evolves freely while the
boundary zone is overwritten by the parent solution, a zero-width version
of the relaxation zone of \citet{davies1976}, with the known risks of
lateral-boundary errors \citep{warner1997}; graph-based neural weather
models have been built for limited areas with boundary forcing
\citep{oskarsson2023}. Training on multi-step rollouts stabilizes learned
solvers \citep{brandstetter2022,lippe2023}, but squared-error rollouts
blur the forecast toward the conditional mean
\citep{lam2023graphcast,bonavita2024}, objectives that match invariant
measures or dynamical invariants have been proposed to counter it
\citep{jiang2023,schiff2024,platt2023}, and training with input noise is
equivalent to a Jacobian penalty \citep{bishop1995}.
Section~\ref{sec:objective} connects both effects to an underestimate of
$\DKY$.

\subsection{What is and is not new here}
\label{sec:bg_novelty}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

That KS admits localized reduced models is not new:
\citet{wittenberg1999} showed with wavelets that its dynamics are
localized in both space and scale, and \citet{wittenberg2001} built
localized low-dimensional models from short periodized subsystems,
arguing that they are minimal models for arbitrarily large domains. That
learned models can reproduce Lyapunov spectra, and that local models can be
run on larger domains, are each established. Table~\ref{tab:prior_work}
summarizes the closest work. What we add is the combination: a learned,
weight-shared local latent field whose zero-shot extensions are checked
against the true Lyapunov spectra at several domain sizes in three
systems; one training procedure, a forced halo, that turns a small
periodic box or windows of one simulation into the same limited-area
problem; an observed limit of the small box, in Kolmogorov flow, with its
signature in free rollouts; and an account of the training choices that
make a learned local rule fail. Showing that a dimension transfers is a
weaker test than it may seem: a local model is extensive by construction
(Remark~\ref{rem:extensive_model}), so wherever the truth is extensive,
scaling the native-size truth by the domain size does as well
(Tables~\ref{tab:transfer1d} and~\ref{tab:transfer2d}). The test has
force where the truth departs from extensivity, and we report it there
too.
