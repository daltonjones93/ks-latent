\section{Two dimensions: a small box and its limit}
\label{sec:results2d}
%======================================================================

In two dimensions we train each system by both routes of
Sec.~\ref{sec:crop_training}: on a small periodic box, $8\times8$ sites, with
a propagator of reach three (route a), and on windows of one large
simulation with a propagator of reach six (route b).
Table~\ref{tab:transfer2d} and Fig.~\ref{fig:routes} collect the results.
Errors are signed and relative to the mean of the truth runs of
Table~\ref{tab:truth} (at Kolmogorov $\alpha=0.05$, $1\times$, each
evaluation against the run from its own initial condition); brackets give
the range over evaluations. Each table row also gives the trivial
extensive baseline, the native-size truth scaled by the area or width
ratio, against which any transfer claim has to be read
(Sec.~\ref{sec:bg_novelty}).

%----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{2D Kuramoto--Sivashinsky: a small box suffices}
\label{sec:ks2d_box}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

Three models trained on the periodic $44^2$ box with different random
seeds reproduce the dimension at the training size and at $66^2$, a size
none of them saw. At $44^2$ the mean over five evaluations (three seeds,
three initial conditions for the first) is $81.7$ against the truth's
$82.0$ ($-0.3\%$, range $-2.4$ to $+1.0\%$); at $66^2$ it is $184.1$ against
$188.5$ ($-2.4\%$, range $-3.2$ to $-1.7\%$, six evaluations). The $66^2$
truth runs themselves span $3.7\%$ ($185.6$, $187.5$ and $192.5$); scored against
the run from its own initial condition, the models are $0.4$--$1.6\%$ low
from one initial condition and $3.7$--$5.1\%$ low from the other. At $88^2$, four
times the training area, the three seeds give $325.5$--$329.6$ against
$338.3$ for the single truth run ($-2.6$ to $-3.8\%$, mean $-3.1\%$). The models
keep the density of the $44^2$ truth, $82.0$ per $44^2$ box; the $88^2$
truth's is $84.6$, so the error here is that of the extensive baseline
($-3.0\%$), and it is of the order of the $3.7\%$ spread among the three $66^2$
truth runs, which a single run cannot resolve. The leading exponent is too high, as for every 2D model we
trained at the training size: $0.216$ against $0.182$ at $44^2$ ($+18\%$) and
$0.219$ against $0.203$ at $66^2$ ($+8\%$; Table~\ref{tab:lambda1}). At $88^2$
it is $0.226$ against $0.255$ ($-11\%$); the single $88^2$ truth run has a higher
$\lambda_1$ than the smaller boxes, which we cannot attribute to the box
or to the run, and the models' stays at $0.22$--$0.24$. Matching $\DKY$
with an excess $\lambda_1$ means the model's spectrum differs in shape: it
has fewer but faster unstable directions, $40$--$42$ positive exponents at
$44^2$ against $42$--$44$ for the truth and $161$--$165$ at $88^2$ against
$174$, with the excess growth compensated
further down the spectrum. The Kolmogorov small-box model at $\alpha=0.1$
shows the same pattern more strongly, $6$--$7$ positive exponents per box
against $7$--$8$.

Two qualifications apply. First, the trivial extensive baseline does as
well: $2.25$ times the $44^2$ truth is within $2.1\%$ of the $66^2$ truth
mean, and four times it is $3.0\%$ below the $88^2$ truth, as far as the
small-box models. The 2D KS truth is extensive at these sizes, so the transfer of
$\DKY$ shows that the local rule has the right density, not more
(Remark~\ref{rem:extensive_model}). Second, the training recipe was chosen
with a test-size truth (Sec.~\ref{sec:canonical_recipe}): the first
small-box recipe, C, gives the same numbers for two of three seeds
($82.5$, $184.3$ and $327.7$ for the first seed, each from three initial
conditions) but $10\%$ low at $66^2$ for the third, and C2, with six times
the box data and longer rollouts, was adopted after comparing both on one
$66^2$ initial condition. All three recipes and seeds are in
Appendix~\ref{app:tables}.

The window-trained model (route b) gives $83.3$ ($+1.6\%$), $188.1$
($-0.3\%$) and $332.7$ ($-1.6\%$) at the three sizes. Its training windows,
$40\times40$ sites of a $264^2$ domain, are larger than every evaluation
domain, so its result shows that a rule learned inside a large domain
also runs on small periodic ones; the small-box models are the test of
training small and running large. Window training alone does not
guarantee it: an earlier window model with full reach and $\kmax=12$
gives $215.9$ at $88^2$, $36\%$ below the truth and about a third below every other model, on a
domain smaller than its own training windows (Appendix~\ref{app:tables}).

\para{The earlier box-trained models.} Our first 2D models were also
trained on the whole $8\times8$-site box, but with a different
architecture (encoder and decoder reaching six sites, $c=3$, propagator
reach six, $\kmax=12$). They miss the dimension by $14\%$ at $44^2$ and $23\%$
at $66^2$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:routes}(a)). We do not know which difference
causes this. It is not that a propagator of reach six reads its own
periodic image on an eight-site box: a control with that reach on the
whole periodic box, in the current architecture, gives $83.3$ at $44^2$ and
$182.9$ at $66^2$ ($+1.6\%$ and $-3.0\%$, one evaluation each).

\subsection{Kolmogorov flow: where the small box stops}
\label{sec:kolm_box}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

At the strongest drag, $\alpha=0.1$, we trained five small-box models:
recipe C with three seeds, and recipes C2 (four times the box data,
longer rollouts) and C3 (C2 with the whole box scored at every step).
The first seed, evaluated from three initial conditions, reproduces the
dimension of its own box and of twice its width: $13.7$ against $14.6$
($-6.4\%$) at $1\times$ and $29.7$ against $30.9$ ($-3.7\%$, range $-5.4$ to
$-1.4\%$) at $2\times$. The other four models, from one initial condition
each, give $-2.4\%$, $-24.5\%$, $-13.1\%$ and $-11.7\%$ at $2\times$: two of the
five are within $4\%$ there, so transfer to twice the width depends on the
seed and the recipe. At three times the width every model misses: the
first seed by $21\%$ ($42.4$ against $53.5$; $-15$ to $-27\%$ over its three
initial conditions), C2 and C3 by $34\%$ and $25\%$. At $\alpha=0.05$ the small-box model
is accurate at $1\times$ ($22.3$ against the matched truth's $22.5$) and
fails at $2\times$ ($25.4$ against $48.6$, $-48\%$); recipe C2 ($28.3$, $-42\%$
at $2\times$; $30.3$, $-60\%$ at $3\times$) and a variant that scores the whole
box at every step ($-39\%$ and $-64\%$) fail in the same way. More data and
longer rollouts do not remove the failure. The extensive baseline, which
like the small-box model assumes the native density, is also low at the
sizes where the truth's density rises ($-18\%$ at $\alpha=0.1$, $3\times$;
$-11\%$ and $-14\%$ at $\alpha=0.05$), but less so than the failing models.

Free rollouts show what goes wrong (Fig.~\ref{fig:routes}(d--f)). At the
sizes where the small-box model fails, it drifts into a state with too
much energy at the largest scales and too little chaos at the forcing
scale. At $\alpha=0.05$ and $2\times$, $42$--$44\%$ of its kinetic energy sits
at $|\bm k|<2$, against $15\%$ in the PDE run from the same initial
condition, and its vorticity standard deviation is $1.15$--$1.23$ against
$1.41$; at $3\times$ the share reaches $61\%$ against $22\%$, and the standard
deviation $0.92$ against $1.43$. Its leading exponent falls to $0.070$
against the truth's $0.111$, and it has 12 positive exponents against
23--26. At $\alpha=0.1$ the first seed drifts only at $3\times$ ($26\%$ against
$19\%$; standard deviation $1.32$ against $1.57$), while at $1\times$ and $2\times$
its rollout statistics match the truth's; recipe C2, which misses at
$2\times$, already drifts there ($22\%$ against $8\%$). The failure thus appears where
the truth's own large-scale energy grows with the channel width
(Sec.~\ref{sec:kolmogorov}): beyond $2\times$ at $\alpha=0.1$, already at $2\times$
at $\alpha=0.05$.

\para{A mechanism consistent with the data.} 2D KS is also not
size-independent at large scales, and still transfers: its share of
energy at $|\bm k|<0.35$ grows from $0.38$ to $0.51$ between $44^2$ and $88^2$,
and the longest wavelengths of a box twice as large carry
$2.7$ times the spectral density of the small box's longest
wavelengths, the same ratio as Kolmogorov flow at $\alpha=0.1$
($2.75$).\footnote{Ratio of the time-averaged spectral density per mode,
three initial conditions per system; it does not separate the systems
that transfer from those that do not, so we do not use it as a test.} How
much energy sits beyond the box therefore does not decide transfer; what
the local dynamics do with it may. Only gradients of $u$ enter the KS
equation, and the gradients of the largest scales are small; we assume,
but have not measured, that this keeps their influence local. In
vorticity form, the velocity that advects $\omega$ at a point is set by
the vorticity everywhere, through the Biot--Savart law, so a model that
sees vorticity within a finite reach must infer the rest from what it
learned in training. We measured how much of the true velocity variance
is set by vorticity farther than the model's reach of three sites. From
$1\times$ to $2\times$ this fraction grows by half at $\alpha=0.1$ (from $0.021$
to $0.033$), doubles at $\alpha=0.05$ ($0.021$ to $0.042$), and grows $2.7$-fold
at $\alpha=0.025$ ($0.018$ to $0.049$; two initial conditions
each); at $3\times$ it is $0.041$, $0.049$ and $0.054$. At the two drags where
small-box models were trained, the failure is milder and later where the
fraction is smaller, but the boundary is not sharp: at $\alpha=0.1$ and
$2\times$, where it is $0.033$, three of five models already miss by
$12$--$25\%$, and we trained no small-box model at $\alpha=0.025$. The fraction stays
below $5\%$, however, and we have not shown that this missing velocity is
what drives the drift, so we offer it as a mechanism consistent with the
data, not as an explanation, and we have no test that predicts the
failure before training. A model that carries the large-scale velocity as
local state, or a reach of six to eight sites, which a window model can
have but an eight-site box cannot, would be the natural tests. We tried the first in one form: two memory channels per
site, written only by the propagator from its neighbours' state and carried forward in time. They
lowered the in-box forecast error by a quarter to a third and the $2\times$ error at
$\alpha=0.05$ from $-39\%$ to $-26\%$, but did not make the small box transfer at $3\times$
at either drag.%

\subsection{Kolmogorov flow: windows of a large simulation}
\label{sec:window_fix}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

Trained on windows of one channel sixteen boxes wide, the same
architecture with reach six transfers at the two stronger drags
(Table~\ref{tab:transfer2d}): the error is $+9.0\%$, $+5.9\%$ and $-2.6\%$ at
$1\times$, $2\times$ and $3\times$ at $\alpha=0.1$, and $+7.9\%$, $-4.8\%$ and $-2.2\%$ at
$\alpha=0.05$. These models differ from the small-box ones in two ways at
once, the training data and the propagator's reach (six sites against
three), and models of reach one fail even on windows of the evaluation
domain (Appendix~\ref{app:tables}). A window model with reach three,
trained on the same data, separates the two.\todo{A2: reach-3 window model
running; state whether the data or the reach makes the difference} Single evaluations range more widely ($-19$ to $+20\%$), as single
truth runs do (Sec.~\ref{sec:truth_uncertainty}). The window models are
themselves nearly extensive: their density per box rises by $9\%$ from
$1\times$ to $3\times$ at $\alpha=0.1$ ($16.0$ to $17.4$) and $5\%$ at $\alpha=0.05$
($23.3$ to $24.5$), against the truth's $22\%$ and $16\%$, and they match the
truth at $3\times$ partly because they are $8$--$9\%$ high at $1\times$. Within the
uncertainty of single truth runs they neither clearly follow nor clearly
miss the truth's rise. At $\alpha=0.025$ it is $19\%$, $8\%$ and
$14\%$ low, with the native size scattering from $13$ to $27$ across initial
conditions; the weakest drag remains the hardest, and its truths, single
40-unit runs that we found biased high by about $9\%$ on average
(Appendix~\ref{app:groundtruth}), may account for part of this. The window
models' free rollouts drift less than the failing small-box models' but
are not free of drift: at $\alpha=0.05$ the large-scale share is $0.19$, $0.25$
and $0.20$ at $1\times$, $2\times$ and $3\times$ against the truth's $0.10$, $0.15$
and $0.22$, with the vorticity's standard deviation within $2\%$; at
$\alpha=0.025$ it is $0.38$ against $0.28$ at $2\times$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:routes}(e,f)).

The caveat that applies to the 2D KS window model applies here with more
force: the training windows are 48 sites wide, six boxes, larger than
every evaluation channel, and the parent simulation is sixteen boxes
wide. These models show that one set of weights serves every channel
width, not that a model trained small runs large; that test, in
Kolmogorov flow, is the one the small box fails beyond twice its width.
The leading exponent is again too high at the two stronger drags
($+16$ to $+30\%$ at $1\times$ and $2\times$, within $6\%$ at $3\times$), and too low at
$\alpha=0.025$ ($-5$ to $-18\%$; Table~\ref{tab:lambda1}).

The window recipe was reached in steps that we report because they
qualify it. A per-patch autoencoder needs no crop margin, at the price of
a reconstruction error about $30\%$ higher in 2D KS; at $\alpha=0.05$, with
$c=6$, it is as accurate in $\DKY$ as a full-reach $c=3$ model, while removing
only the decoder's reach at $c=3$ gives errors that grow with size
(Fig.~\ref{fig:appendix_panels}(b)). Short rollouts can leave the
free-running model under-constrained and long ones lower $\DKY$
(Secs.~\ref{sec:fail_regimes} and~\ref{sec:fail_mse}), so the rollout length
differs between the window models ($\kmax=32$ for 2D KS and $\alpha=0.025$,
$16$ at $\alpha=0.1$, $12$ at $\alpha=0.05$; a $\kmax=32$ retraining at
$\alpha=0.05$ gives comparable errors, Appendix~\ref{app:tables}).

\subsection{Choosing among variants without the truth: a case study}
\label{sec:canonical_results}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

At $\alpha=0.1$ we trained six window variants that differ in $\kmax$
($16$--$32$) and in the autoencoder's regularizers (Sec.~\ref{sec:fail_mse}),
whose errors against the truth trade off between sizes. To avoid choosing
on the reference, we wrote a rule that uses no Lyapunov truth
(Appendix~\ref{app:architecture}): a variant is eligible if its free
rollouts reproduce the training data's vorticity variance and
forcing-band energy share to within $15\%$ at every size, and the eligible
variant with the lowest physical-space forecast error, eight steps ahead
on the held-out validation trajectory, is selected. The chronology
matters. The rule was written after every variant had been scored
against the truths then available, and after an earlier choice had been
made for its $3\times$ accuracy, although the rule's metric itself had not
been computed. All six variants were eligible (largest deviation
$6.3\%$), so the free-rollout screen selected nothing; the forecast error
decided, and the selected variant's is lower than every other's by at
least 4.6 paired standard errors, within a total spread of $7\%$
(Table~\ref{tab:alpha01_ablation}).

Against the truths available when the rule was applied, the selected
variant had the lowest mean error of the six. With the two $1\times$ truth
runs added since, which lowered the $1\times$ reference from $15.9$ to $14.6$, it
is $+9.0\%$, $+5.9\%$ and $-2.6\%$ at the three widths and ranks third: the
mean absolute errors of the six variants span $4.7$--$7.7\%$, a range smaller
than the spread of single evaluations and of the truth runs. Across the
six variants the forecast error and the mean $\DKY$ error were strongly
rank-correlated against the earlier truths. We read this as one case in
which a cheap, truth-free forecast metric picked a variant among the
better ones, not as an established selection rule; the ranking of these
variants is not resolved by the available truths.

\subsection{Scaling of the dimension with domain size}
\label{sec:scaling}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

Figure~\ref{fig:scaling} shows $\DKY$ as a function of domain size. For 2D
KS the dimension per $44^2$ box of the small-box models is $81.7$, $81.8$ and
$82.0$ at the three sizes, and that of the window model $83.2$--$83.6$; the
truth's is $82.0$ and $83.8$ at the two sizes where it exists. The $2.2\%$
difference between the truth's two values is inside the spread of the
individual runs ($80.7$--$83.6$ at $44^2$; $82.5$--$85.6$ per box at $66^2$),
so the truth is consistent with extensive scaling, and so are both models,
as Remark~\ref{rem:extensive_model} requires. In Kolmogorov flow the truth
is not extensive (Sec.~\ref{sec:kolmogorov}). The density per box of the
first small-box model is nearly flat ($13.7$, $14.9$, $14.1$ at $\alpha=0.1$)
and falls behind the truth's rise at $3\times$; the window-trained models
rise less than the truth ($5$--$9\%$ against $16$--$22\%$) but stay within the
spread of the truth runs at $\alpha=0.1$ and $0.05$, and fall below the
rising truth at $\alpha=0.025$ ($23.7$ per box against $27.6$ at $3\times$).

\para{Beyond the dimension.} The decoded free rollouts of the 2D KS models
keep the field amplitude close to the truth's but do not follow the
slow roughening of its largest scales. From three initial conditions,
the true field's standard deviation grows from $3.7$ at $44^2$ to $4.0$ at
$66^2$ and $4.3$ at $88^2$, and the share of its energy at $|\bm k|<0.35$
from $0.38$ to $0.46$ and $0.51$. The small-box model (first seed) stays
nearly flat instead: its standard deviation is $0.97$--$1.12$, $0.95$--$0.98$
and $0.87$--$0.92$ times the truth's at the three sizes, and its
large-scale share is $0.49$, $0.45$ and $0.42$. The window model drifts the
other way, its standard deviation $1.03$, $1.11$ and $1.19$ times the
truth's at the three sizes (Table~\ref{tab:spectra}). Neither defect shows
in $\DKY$, consistent with the large scales of KS entering the dynamics
only through their small gradients. Both models normalize by the standard
deviation of their training data, $3.8$ on the $44^2$ boxes and $6.2$ on
the $264^2$ domain. The model's anchor channel carries the patch mean of $u$, a mode
the truth projects out, and we have not removed the corresponding
direction from the model's spectrum; with $\DKY\approx80$ at $44^2$ one
spurious near-neutral exponent would change it by about one percent.
For the window model at $\alpha=0.1$ the kinetic energy around the forcing
wavenumber ($2\le|\bm k|<8$) is within $10\%$ of the truth's at every width
and the vorticity's standard deviation within $3\%$. The per-patch
decoder also leaves seams at patch edges: it puts $1.7$--$2.4\%$ of the 2D
KS energy at $|\bm k|\ge1.4$, against $0.5$--$0.8\%$ in the truth
(Fig.~\ref{fig:rollouts}(d--f)), already present in the reconstruction of
the true field, so they come from the decoder rather than the dynamics; in
data assimilation, averaging over shifted copies of the patch lattice
removes them \citep{jones2026da}. Full tables are in
Appendix~\ref{app:tables}.
