%======================================================================
\section{Systems, data and ground truth}
\label{sec:systems}
%======================================================================

We use three periodic, scalar, spatiotemporally chaotic systems of
increasing difficulty: the one-dimensional Kuramoto--Sivashinsky (KS)
equation, its two-dimensional isotropic generalization, and
two-dimensional Kolmogorov flow with linear drag. All three are
integrated in Fourier space with a fourth-order exponential time-differencing
Runge--Kutta scheme (ETDRK4), with the coefficients evaluated by the
contour-integral method of \citet{kassam2005} (32 points), in double
precision on the CPU. Table~\ref{tab:systems} collects the parameters,
Table~\ref{tab:truth} the ground-truth Kaplan--Yorke dimensions, and
Appendix~\ref{app:groundtruth} the full protocol and the solver
validation. Figure~\ref{fig:systems} shows each system on the domain its
models are trained on.

\subsection{One-dimensional Kuramoto--Sivashinsky}
\label{sec:ks1d}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

The KS equation \citep{kuramoto1976,sivashinsky1977},
\begin{equation}
  u_t + u u_x + u_{xx} + u_{xxxx} = 0, \qquad x\in[0,L),
  \label{eq:ks1d}
\end{equation}
has a linear growth rate $k^2-k^4$ that is positive for $|k|<1$ and
largest at $k=1/\sqrt2$, which sets a pattern wavelength of
$2\sqrt2\,\pi\approx8.9$. For $L$ large compared with this length the
dynamics are extensively chaotic, with $\DKY\approx0.226L$ minus a
small offset \citep{manneville1985,edson2019}. We train at $L=100$ on a
grid of $256$ points ($\Delta x = 0.39$), with solver step $0.05$ and
snapshot interval $\Delta t=1.0$. The training set has 60 independent
trajectories (50 training, 10 validation) of 250 time units each, after
a spin-up of 500 time units.

Ground truth is computed at $\Delta x = 100/1024$, four times finer than
the training grid, to keep the solver error well below the model error,
with averaging windows of 1200--2000 time units (100 or more Lyapunov
times for $L\ge50$). At $L=100$ the reference value is $\DKY=22.5$,
inside the range $[21,24]$ reported in the literature, with
$\lambda_1=0.090$ and 12 positive exponents; \citet{edson2019} give
$\DKY\approx0.226L-0.16$ for $L\gtrsim80$, i.e.\ $22.4$. At $L=22$ our
solver gives $\DKY=5.26$ and $\lambda_1=0.049$, against $5.20$ and $0.043$
from \citet{edson2019} and a leading exponent of $0.048$ from
\citet{cvitanovic2010}. The transfer sizes $L=25,50,200,400$ give the
densities in Table~\ref{tab:truth}: $\DKY/L$ lies between $0.224$ and
$0.226$ for every $L\ge50$, while at $L=25$, close to the onset of
chaos, $\lambda_1\approx0.001$ and the density falls to about $0.07$.

\subsection{Two-dimensional Kuramoto--Sivashinsky}
\label{sec:ks2d}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

The isotropic 2D generalization,
\begin{equation}
  u_t + \nabla^2 u + \nabla^4 u + \tfrac12|\nabla u|^2 = 0,
  \qquad (x,y)\in[0,L)^2,
  \label{eq:ks2d}
\end{equation}
has the same linear operator, now unstable on the ring $|\bm k|<1$
\citep{kalogirou2015}. Unlike the 1D equation, its nonlinearity is not a
total derivative and drives the spatial mean of $u$. The mean carries
no dynamical information (only gradients of $u$ enter the right-hand
side), so we project the $\bm k=0$ mode out after every step, in the
base trajectory and in every tangent vector. All runs use solver step
$0.02$, snapshot interval $\Delta t=0.1$ and grid spacing
$\Delta x = 0.344$ ($128$ points at $L=44$).

The small-box models (route a) are trained on 144 trajectories (plus six
for validation) of 25 time units on the periodic $44^2$ box ($128^2$
points), each after a spin-up of 200 time units. The window-trained
models (route b) are trained on four trajectories (plus one) of 25 time
units on a single periodic domain of side $264=6\times44$ ($768^2$
points). Both are evaluated on square periodic boxes of side $44$, $66$
and $88$. Ground truth exists at $44^2$ (three runs, $\DKY=80.7$--$83.6$,
mean $82.0$, 42--44 positive exponents) and $66^2$ (three runs, $185.6$, $187.5$ and
$192.5$, mean $188.5$, 93--101 positive exponents). The
mean densities, $0.0424$ and $0.0433$ per unit area, differ by $2.2\%$,
inside the spread of the individual runs. At $88^2$ a single truth run with 400
tangent directions gives $\DKY=338.3$ with 174 positive exponents, so
the 400 directions bracket it; its density, $0.0437$ per unit area, is
$0.9\%$ above the $66^2$ mean and $3.1\%$ above the $44^2$ mean.

The field's own statistics are not the same on every box: its standard
deviation is $3.8$ on the $44^2$ boxes and $6.2$ on the $264^2$ domain,
and in free runs of the true PDE it grows from $3.7$ at $44^2$ to $4.3$ at
$88^2$, with the share of energy at $|\bm k|<0.35$ rising from $0.38$ to
$0.51$ (means over three initial conditions). Only gradients of $u$ enter
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ks2d}, so we expect this slow roughening of the largest scales
\citep[the long-wavelength roughening studied by][]{jayaprakash1993}
to leave the local dynamics nearly unchanged (an assumption we have not
measured), and the dimension density is indeed nearly unchanged ($+3.1\%$ from $44^2$ to $88^2$); the two routes normalize their fields by their own training
statistics.

\subsection{Kolmogorov flow with linear drag}
\label{sec:kolmogorov}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

Two-dimensional incompressible flow in vorticity form,
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
  \omega_t + \bm u\cdot\nabla\omega = \nu\nabla^2\omega - \alpha\,\omega + f(y),\\
  \bm u = (\psi_y,-\psi_x),\qquad \nabla^2\psi=-\omega,
\end{gathered}
  \label{eq:kolmogorov}
\end{equation}
on $[0,L_x)\times[0,2\pi)$, with forcing
$f(y) = -(\nu n^2+\alpha)\,n\cos(ny)$. This forcing makes
$\bm u = (\sin ny, 0)$ an exact steady solution for every $\alpha$, which
gives an exact test of the solver (Appendix~\ref{app:groundtruth}).
We use $n=4$ and $\nu=0.01$, and three drag coefficients
$\alpha\in\{0.025,0.05,0.1\}$. We quote a Reynolds number
$Re=\sqrt{f_0}/\nu$ built from the velocity forcing amplitude
$f_0=\nu n^2+\alpha$ on a $2\pi$ box; this is the definition of
\citet{kolmogorovregimes2026} applied to our forcing, and coincides with
theirs for $\alpha=0$ (their flows have no drag). Our three drags give
$Re\approx43$, $46$ and $51$.

The drag is what makes the flow usable for domain transfer. Without it,
the two-dimensional inverse energy cascade accumulates energy at the
largest scale the domain allows
\citep{kraichnan1967,smithyakhot1993,boffettaecke2012}, so the
statistics depend on the domain size. With drag, and at the moderate
Reynolds numbers used here, the flow is still not extensive at the sizes
we test. The truth's dimension per $2\pi\times2\pi$ box is $14.6$, $15.4$
and $17.8$ at $1\times$, $2\times$ and $3\times$ for $\alpha=0.1$, $21.5$,
$24.3$ and $25.1$ for $\alpha=0.05$, and $25.2$, $25.8$ and $27.6$ for
$\alpha=0.025$ (Table~\ref{tab:truth}): it rises by $9$--$22\%$ from one
box to three, at every drag, by amounts comparable to the uncertainty of
single truth runs (Sec.~\ref{sec:truth_uncertainty}). The share of kinetic
energy at $|\bm k|<2$ in free runs of the PDE shows the same trend more
directly (one initial condition, 200 time units): $0.087$, $0.080$ and
$0.189$ at $\alpha=0.1$; $0.096$, $0.146$ and $0.215$ at $\alpha=0.05$; and
$0.144$ and $0.277$ at $1\times$ and $2\times$ for $\alpha=0.025$. Large scales
gain energy as the channel widens, more at weaker drag; at $\alpha=0.1$
this sets in only beyond twice the box. We therefore do not treat
Kolmogorov flow at these sizes as extensive, and compare models with the
truth size by size.

The forcing singles out $y$: the equation is translation-invariant in
$x$ and invariant in $y$ only under shifts by the forcing period $2\pi/n$.
We therefore keep the height fixed at $2\pi$ and grow the domain in $x$
only, to $2\times$ and $3\times$ the square box. Kolmogorov results are
statements about widening the channel, not about growing its area in
both directions; the 2D KS results are the isotropic test. All runs use
grid spacing $2\pi/128$, solver step $0.01$ and snapshot interval
$\Delta t=0.2$. The window-trained models use a domain of width
$16\times2\pi$ ($2048\times128$ points), four training and one validation
trajectory of 40 time units per drag value, after a spin-up of 100 time
units. The small-box models use the $2\pi\times2\pi$ box ($128^2$ points),
24 training and six validation trajectories of 40 time units (recipe C),
or 96 and six (recipe C2).

\subsection{How precise is the ground truth?}
\label{sec:truth_uncertainty}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

Every comparison in this paper is between a model's $\DKY$ and a
finite-time estimate of the true $\DKY$ from one or a few initial
conditions, and the precision of that estimate limits what can be
claimed (details in Appendix~\ref{app:groundtruth}). In 1D KS, windows of
1200--2000 time units span 100 or more Lyapunov times; independent
finite-time realizations agree to within $0.1$--$2\%$ for $L\ge50$, and we
treat the 1D truths as accurate to about one percent. The 2D KS truths
use 40-unit windows (about 7 Lyapunov times); independent initial
conditions agree to within about $\pm2\%$ of their mean, and the running estimates still move by
$3$--$5\%$ over the last 20 time units, so these truths are uncertain at
the level of a few percent. In Kolmogorov flow, Lyapunov times are 6--16 time
units, and a 40-unit window covers only 2--6 of them: for seven runs
continued to 120 units, the 40-unit value was off by $-17\%$ to $+23\%$,
high in five of the seven, and independent initial conditions at a fixed
size and window differ by $4$--$12\%$
(Appendix~\ref{app:truth_convergence}).

Where several truth runs exist for one configuration we use their mean
(Table~\ref{tab:truth}). At $\alpha=0.05$, $1\times$, the three 120-unit
truths start from the same initial conditions as the model evaluations,
and we score each model run against its matched truth, which removes
truth-side noise only (Fig.~\ref{fig:appendix_panels}(a)). At
$\alpha=0.1$, $1\times$, the three runs span $12.9$--$15.9$, a $21\%$
spread around their mean. Four Kolmogorov configurations ($\alpha=0.05$,
$3\times$; all three sizes at $\alpha=0.025$) rest on a single 40-unit
run. We therefore report the Kolmogorov
results as scaling of $\DKY$ with domain size, with the spread above as
the truth's error bar, and treat differences of 10--15\% between a model
and a single 40-unit truth as within the uncertainty of the reference.
Model and truth also average over different windows (2000 model steps,
against 40 or 120 time units for the truth), and short truth windows are
biased high on average (Appendix~\ref{app:truth_convergence}), which tilts
the comparison toward negative model errors by a few percent. The 1D
results carry no such caveat.
