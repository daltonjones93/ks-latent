\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
%======================================================================

\para{What a transfer test can show.} A local model is extensive by
construction (Remark~\ref{rem:extensive_model}), so once its density is
right at the training size, its dimension on a larger domain follows
almost automatically wherever the truth is extensive. In 1D KS and 2D KS
the trivial baseline that scales the native truth by the domain size is
as accurate as the models (Tables~\ref{tab:transfer1d}
and~\ref{tab:transfer2d}). There, transfer of $\DKY$ shows that the learned
rule has the right density of chaos and stays chaotic on larger lattices,
which is necessary but weaker than showing the rule is the right one. The
test discriminates where the truth is not extensive. In Kolmogorov flow
the truth's density per box rises with the channel width, and there the
models separate: the small-box model stays near its native density and
misses, while the window-trained model follows the rise. Finite-size
departures from extensivity \citep{fishmanegolf2006}, comoving exponents
\citep{deisslerkaneko1987} and correlation functions
\citep{egolfgreenside1994} after transfer would sharpen the test further; we have not
computed them for the models.

\para{What the training data must contain.} Trained on a small periodic
box, the local rule transferred in 1D and 2D KS and failed in Kolmogorov
flow beyond the width at which the truth's large-scale energy starts to
grow. 2D KS is also not size-independent at its largest scales, so the
amount of large-scale energy outside the box cannot be the whole story;
the evidence we have is consistent with the difference lying in how the
local dynamics depend on those scales, through gradients in KS and through
the non-local Biot--Savart velocity in vorticity form
(Sec.~\ref{sec:kolm_box}). We found no truth-side statistic that predicts
the failure before training. Windows of one large simulation avoid the
problem at the price of the large simulation; they are the setting of a
limited-area model nested in a larger one, and the forced halo trains
exactly that problem (Proposition~\ref{prop:halo}). Rollouts must also
leave the learned map chaotic at the right rate
(Secs.~\ref{sec:fail_regimes} and~\ref{sec:fail_mse}); free-rollout
statistics detect spurious regimes without any truth, and in one case a
short-horizon forecast error picked a variant among the better ones
(Sec.~\ref{sec:canonical_results}).

\para{The interaction range and the reach.} The light cone is far too
short to constrain the reach of a model at these time steps; the length
that matters is the distance beyond which neighbouring values stop
improving a one-step prediction, three to four sites for the 1D latent
dynamics against a trained reach of six (Sec.~\ref{sec:receptive}), a
measurement made on coordinates that already reach six sites. We did not
measure it in 2D. In Kolmogorov flow most of the unstable directions lie
in the band around the forcing wavenumber, $31.5$ of $36$ at
$|\bm k|\in[2,8)$ for $\alpha=0.05$ at $3\times$, whose wavelengths span one to
four sites (Appendix~\ref{app:chaos_scale}), and models whose encoder
reaches two sites and whose propagator reaches one fail even when trained
on windows of the very domain they are evaluated on, with errors of
$66$--$100\%$ in $\DKY$ (Appendix~\ref{app:tables}). The velocity that
advects the vorticity is a longer-range quantity: in the true flow at
twice the box width, vorticity farther than six to eight sites sets less
than one percent of its variance, more than a small box can supply. The
reach also sets the smallest domain the model can be used on, which is
what fails at $L=25$.

\para{Learned baselines.} A global latent model of the same system, such
as a 44-dimensional one we trained at $L=100$ in 1D ($\DKY=21.4$ against the
truth's $22.5$), cannot be evaluated at another size at all: its bottleneck and propagator are tied to one
latent dimension, so the comparison that matters for transfer has no
global counterpart. Whether a \emph{latent} field buys anything over a
weight-shared local surrogate in physical space, such as a learned coarse
simulator \citep{kochkov2021,stachenfeld2022}, is a separate question,
which the Lyapunov protocol used here could answer and which we leave
open; Table~\ref{tab:prior_work} records only that such models have not
been judged by their spectra. Our latent's specific advantage is the one
the companion paper uses, a per-site state on which a localized ensemble
filter operates exactly \citep{jones2026da}.

\para{The parameters describe the law, not the state.} Because every
operator is shared by all sites, the trained parameters encode one local
rule, and their number is fixed by that rule rather than by the domain.
A 2D model of about $76{,}000$ parameters, trained on a $44^2$ box,
reproduces a Kaplan--Yorke dimension of $189$ at $66^2$ to within $3\%$
(Table~\ref{tab:transfer2d}), and
a 12-channel version runs a $440^2$ domain, whose attractor dimension we
estimate at roughly $8{,}000$ from the measured density of $0.043$ per unit area
\citep{jones2026da}. The same weight sharing makes training cheap in
data: every window, and every site within it, is a training example of
the same rule. Weight sharing and size independence are not new by
themselves \citep{barsinai2019,kochkov2021}; what the dimension comparison
adds is evidence that the shared rule carries the right density of chaos
when it is extended to a larger domain. A
system made of many copies of one local process is described most
economically by learning that process once.

\para{Transfer error as a probe of the physics.} Because a local model
is extensive by construction, the difference between its dimension
density and the truth's on a larger domain measures how far the truth is
from extensive at that size, once the model is accurate at the native
size. The rise of the Kolmogorov truth's density with channel width, and
the matching failure of the small-box model, are an example. Deviations
from extensivity have been studied as a probe of a system's building
blocks before; a learned local model offers a way to ask the same
question of systems known only through data, which we leave to future
work.

\para{Limitations.} We state them plainly.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Ground truth.} Four Kolmogorov configurations rest on one
  40-unit run, uncertain by 10--15\%, the $\alpha=0.1$ native-size truth
  runs spread by $21\%$, and the 2D KS truths are uncertain by a few
  percent (Sec.~\ref{sec:truth_uncertainty}).\todo{88$^2$ truth: drop or
  update the clause on 2D KS at $88^2$} Model and truth average over
  different windows.
\item \emph{Leading exponents.} In 2D the models' $\lambda_1$ is $12$--$30\%$
  too high at most sizes even where $\DKY$ agrees
  (Table~\ref{tab:lambda1}), and in 1D $4$--$9\%$; the spectra match in their
  integral, not in every exponent. For data assimilation, where $\lambda_1$
  sets error growth, this matters directly.
\item \emph{Kolmogorov geometry and route.} The channel is widened in $x$
  only, because the forcing fixes the height; transfer in $y$ is untested.
  The Kolmogorov models that transfer were trained on windows larger than
  every evaluation domain.
\item \emph{Models and seeds.} The 1D model has three training seeds and
  the 2D KS small-box model three; the Kolmogorov window models have one
  (the $\alpha=0.05$ model two).\todo{window-model seeds 1--2 at all three
  drags are running: update this item and Table~\ref{tab:transfer2d}} The
  1D and 2D architectures differ (full reach and a latent coherence
  penalty in 1D, per-patch in 2D), and the 2D KS small-box recipe was
  chosen with a test-size truth (Sec.~\ref{sec:canonical_recipe}).
\item \emph{What the spectra compare.} The model's Lyapunov spectrum is
  that of the latent map. Comparing it with the PDE's assumes that the
  decoder is a smooth, injective map on the attractor, which we have not
  verified. In 1D the latent dimension, $48$, only just exceeds
  $2\DKY+1\approx46$.
\item \emph{Reconstruction and large scales.} Local neural-field latents
  have been reported to fail on KS at the decoder \citep{aroma2024}. Ours
  reconstruct the field to $0.01\%$ (1D), $2.6$--$2.9\%$ (2D KS) and $1.2\%$
  (Kolmogorov) of its variance, with seams at patch edges in 2D, and the
  2D KS models do not reproduce the slow roughening of the largest scales
  exactly (Sec.~\ref{sec:scaling}).
\end{itemize}

\para{Outlook.} The ingredients carry over to any system whose chaos is
extensive and whose dynamics are local at the scale of a few patches:
convection in large aspect-ratio cells, limited-area atmospheric models
nested in a global one, where the forced halo is already the operating
mode, and high-resolution models whose domain is chosen by the
application rather than by the training data. Three questions follow
from this study. Can a small box be made to work where the dynamics
depend on scales it cannot contain, for example by carrying the
large-scale velocity as local state, or must such systems be learned from
windows of a larger simulation? Does transfer hold in a
direction the training data never varied, such as the height of the
Kolmogorov channel? And can the rule be learned from incomplete, noisy
data rather than from complete fields? A companion paper uses the local
latent field for ensemble data assimilation, with a model trained only on
noisy fields \citep{jones2026da}.
