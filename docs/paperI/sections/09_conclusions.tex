%======================================================================
\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}
%======================================================================

A latent field, built only from operators that are shared by all sites
and read a bounded neighbourhood, gives a learned surrogate of extensive
chaos that can be run on domains it was never trained on, and the
models' own Lyapunov exponents show that it reproduces the density of
chaos there. In one dimension the transferred Kuramoto--Sivashinsky model
stays within $0.2$--$1.2\%$ of the true Kaplan--Yorke dimension up to four
times the training length, and its spectrum, not only its dimension,
follows the truth's. In two dimensions, Kuramoto--Sivashinsky models
trained on a small periodic box are within $4\%$ of
the truth at the training size, at $2.25$ times its area and at four times
its area ($-2.6$ to $-3.8\%$ against a single truth run), from three
training seeds; at four times the area the trivial extensive baseline is
$3.0\%$ low.
Their leading exponents are $8$--$18\%$ too high, so the agreement is in
the dimension, not in every exponent.

Kolmogorov flow marks the limit of the small box. Models trained on one
$2\pi\times2\pi$ box reproduce the dimension at twice the channel width at
the strongest drag for two of five training seeds and recipes, and all are
$21$--$34\%$ low at three times; at a weaker drag they fail already at twice
the width, drifting into a state with two to three
times the true large-scale energy; more data and longer rollouts do not
change this. The failure sets in where the truth's own large-scale energy
grows with the domain, and is consistent with the non-local dependence of
vorticity dynamics on the velocity, which a finite reach cannot see. Models
trained on windows of one large simulation, with a forced halo and twice
the propagator reach, are within $10\%$ at the two stronger drags; their
windows are larger than the domains they are evaluated on, so they show
that one set of weights serves every size, not that a small domain
suffices. Window data with the small-box reach of three did not transfer
(dimension $31$--$71\%$ low at $1\times$, $38$--$63\%$ at $3\times$), so the
reach was necessary on windows; reach six without window data was not
tested.

Two further lessons concern training. Rollouts that are too short leave
the free-running model room to settle into spurious regimes, which
free-rollout statistics catch without any truth; in three confounded
comparisons longer squared-error rollouts lowered the dimension, consistent with, though not established
by, the law of total variance applied to rollout targets.

The practice we take from this work is short: train the local rule on a
small box if that is all the data there is (with a forced halo when it is
trained on windows), check the result against the Lyapunov exponents and
the free-rollout statistics of the truth at more than one size, and fall
back to windows of a larger simulation where the small box fails, as it
did in Kolmogorov flow, where the dynamics are consistent with depending on
scales the box cannot contain. The latent field also has a
geometry, which a companion paper uses to localize an ensemble filter in
latent space \citep{jones2026da}.
