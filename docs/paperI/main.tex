\title{Local latent fields for extensive spatiotemporal chaos: zero-shot domain transfer
validated by Lyapunov spectra}

\author{Dalton Jones}
\email{daltjone@colostate.edu}
\author{Peter Jan van Leeuwen}
\affiliation{Department of Atmospheric Science, Colorado State University, Fort Collins, Colorado 80523, USA}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
Learned surrogates of spatiotemporally chaotic systems usually compress the state into one latent
vector whose size is fixed at training time, although in extensive chaos the number of active
degrees of freedom grows with the domain. We make the latent a field: a few channels at each site of
a lattice that tiles the domain, built only from weight-shared operators of finite reach, so that one
model, of $76{,}000$--$135{,}000$ parameters in 2D, runs on a domain of any size by adding sites.
One training procedure serves a small periodic box and windows of one large simulation alike: the
sites bordering the scored region are overwritten with data at every step, so the training loss is
that of a limited-area model. Judged by its own Lyapunov spectrum, a one-dimensional Kuramoto--Sivashinsky model
trained at $L=100$ reproduces the Kaplan--Yorke dimension to within $0.2$--$1.2\%$ up to $L=400$, and
two-dimensional Kuramoto--Sivashinsky models trained on a $44\times44$ periodic box, from three
training seeds, are within $3.5\%$ of it at the training size and at $66^2$, and keep the same
dimension density at four times the training area. % TODO(P1-8): 88^2 truth (running) -> "within x% at 88^2"
In Kolmogorov flow with linear drag, small-box models reproduce the dimension at twice the channel
width at the strongest drag, though not for every training seed, and miss it by $21\%$ at three
times; at a weaker drag they fail already at twice the width, drifting into a state with two to
three times the true large-scale energy. Models trained on windows of a simulation
sixteen boxes wide, wider than every evaluation domain, are within $10\%$ at the two stronger drags,
comparable to the uncertainty of the reference. In two dimensions the leading exponent is
$12$--$30\%$ too high even where the dimension agrees. We also report which training choices make a
learned local rule fail: short rollouts admit spurious free-running regimes, and longer
squared-error rollouts lower the dimension.
\end{abstract}

\maketitle

\begin{quotation}
Many chaotic systems, from flame fronts to atmospheric flows, are spatially extended: the larger
the domain, the more independent chaotic degrees of freedom it holds. Machine-learned surrogates
usually describe the whole state by one list of numbers of fixed length, which cannot grow with
the domain. We instead learn a field of a few numbers at each point of a lattice, updated by the
same local rule everywhere, so that a model trained on one domain runs on a larger one simply by
adding lattice points. We test this transfer with the strictest check available, the model's
own Lyapunov spectrum against that of the true equations, in three systems. A small training box
is enough for the Kuramoto--Sivashinsky equation in one and two dimensions. In Kolmogorov flow it
is not: once the larger domain hosts large-scale motion that the small box cannot contain, the
transferred model drifts, and training on pieces of one large simulation is needed instead.
\end{quotation}
