\subsection{Analysis schemes}
\label{app:da_filters}

Let $Z\in\R^{\Nens\times\dlat}$ be the forecast ensemble of one tiling, $\bar z$ its mean,
$X=Z-\bar z$ the perturbations, $B=X^\top X/(\Nens-1)$, $J$ the Jacobian of $h=\Hobs\circ\Dec$ at
$\bar z$, and $\rho$ the Gaspari--Cohn taper \citep{gaspari1999} of the site distance, of half-width
$1.5$ sites in 2D (zero at 3 sites and beyond) and 3 sites in 1D.

\para{The Part~II filter.} Each tiling runs the deterministic local transform below at every site,
followed by relaxation to prior spread \citep{whitakerhamill2012}: for every latent coordinate $j$,
with forecast and analysis spreads $\sigma^f_j$ and $\sigma^a_j$, the analysis perturbations are
multiplied by $1+\alpha(\sigma^f_j-\sigma^a_j)/\sigma^a_j$, $\alpha=0.7$. The forecast is $\Fp$ applied
five times, each time adding independent Gaussian noise of standard deviation $0.02$ to every latent
coordinate. The initial ensemble of a tiling is its encoding of the initial truth plus independent
noise of standard deviation $0.1$ per coordinate (or, in the robustness test of
Table~\ref{tab:da_robust}, the encodings of 64 states of an independent trajectory, two time units
apart).

\para{Shifted tilings.} Tiling $k$ works on the field translated by $o_k\in\{0,8\}^2$ grid points
(half a 16-point patch): it encodes $\Tr_{o_k}u$, and its observation operator reads the observations
at their translated positions, so all tilings assimilate the same data. Each member $e$ of tiling $k$
is decoded and translated back, $u^{(e)}_k=\Tr_{o_k}^{-1}\Dec(z^{(e)}_k)$, and the blended ensemble is
$u^{(e)}=\sum_k w_k\,u^{(e)}_k$ with the weights of Eq.~\eqref{eq:blend}, pairing member $e$ of every
tiling; its mean is the blended analysis and its spread the reported spread. With uniform weights,
$w_k=1/K$. The blended fields are output only: each tiling's next forecast starts from its own
analysis ensemble.

\para{Filters used in 1D.} The 1D experiments use a single tiling, no RTPS, model noise $0.08$, and
one of the following analyses.

\para{Dense filter.} With perturbed observations
$y^{(e)}=y+\varepsilon^{(e)}$, $\varepsilon^{(e)}\sim\mathcal N(0,R)$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  z_a^{(e)} &= z^{(e)} + K\big(y^{(e)}-h(z^{(e)})\big),\\
  K &= (\rho\circ B)J^\top\big(J(\rho\circ B)J^\top+R\big)^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The innovation uses the nonlinear $h$; the gain uses $J$.

\para{Per-site filter.} For each site $s$, let $U_s$ be the observations
with $\rho(\dist(s,\mathrm{site}(i)))>0$, $N_s$ the sites that own them,
and $R_s=\mathrm{diag}(R_{ii}/\rho_i)_{i\in U_s}$. With $J_s$ the rows of $J$
in $U_s$ restricted to the columns of $N_s$ (exact by
Proposition~\ref{prop:blockdiag}), $B_{N_s}$ the sample covariance of the
sites in $N_s$ and $B_{s,N_s}$ that of site $s$ with them,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  z_{a,s}^{(e)} &= z_s^{(e)} + K_s\big(y^{(e)}_{U_s}-h_{U_s}(z^{(e)})\big),\\
  K_s &= B_{s,N_s}J_s^\top\big(J_sB_{N_s}J_s^\top+R_s\big)^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The covariance $B_{N_s}$ is not tapered; localization acts only through
$R_s$ \citep{hunt2007}.

\para{Deterministic transform.} With the same $U_s$, $R_s$ and
$Y=X_{N_s}J_s^\top\in\R^{\Nens\times|U_s|}$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \tilde P &= \big[(\Nens-1)I+YR_s^{-1}Y^\top\big]^{-1},\\
  \bar w &= \tilde P\,YR_s^{-1}\big(y-\overline{h(z)}\big)_{U_s},\\
  W &= \big[(\Nens-1)\tilde P\big]^{1/2},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
and site $s$'s analysis ensemble is $\bar z_s + (\bar w\mathbf 1^\top+W)^\top X_s$
\citep{hunt2007}. For a linear $h$ without localization it reproduces
the Kalman update computed with the ensemble covariance exactly (unit
test). It is the analysis of every 2D experiment. Its stochastic counterpart, the per-site filter
above, gave the same skill to within $0.15$ at every ensemble size with an earlier single-tiling
filter at $44^2$, at about $40\%$ higher CPU time.

\para{Particle flow filter.} The Gauss--Newton (``NAT'') variant of
\citet{huvanleeuwen2021} with the localized prior covariance $\rho\circ B$,
held fixed in pseudo-time, at most 100 pseudo-time steps.

\para{Sampling error correction.} The correction table of
\citet{anderson2012} is built by Monte Carlo for each ensemble size
(2000 trials per true correlation on a grid of 41 values in
$[-0.98,0.98]$, 100 bins). In \emph{matrix form} each sample correlation in
$B$ is replaced by its corrected value; the result need not be positive
semidefinite. The \emph{projected form} raises its eigenvalues to at
least $10^{-10}$ times the largest. The \emph{serial form} assimilates the
observations one at a time on the augmented state $[Z,h(Z)]$ and applies
the correction to each scalar regression coefficient,
$\beta_k=\mathrm{SEC}\big(\mathrm{corr}(x_k,\hat y_j)\big)\,\sigma_{x_k}\sigma_{\hat y_j}/(\sigma^2_{\hat y_j}+R_{jj})$.

\subsection{The physics-based comparator}
\label{app:da_comparator}

\para{Filter.} The state is the field on the PDE grid ($128^2$ at $44^2$), integrated with ETDRK4 at
$\Delta t=0.02$. The analysis is a local ensemble transform Kalman filter on blocks of $4\times4$ grid
points: every point of a block receives the transform computed at the block centre from the
observations whose Gaspari--Cohn weight, now a function of physical distance with half-width $c$, is
positive, each with its error variance divided by that weight \citep{hunt2007}, followed by RTPS. The
observations, their errors and their random draws are those of the latent experiments. The
64-member initial ensemble is the true initial field plus independent smooth perturbations (Gaussian
correlation over 4 grid points) of standard deviation $0.5$ in standardized units. With the true
equations and its best setting ($c=24$ length units, no RTPS) it reaches $0.076$.

\para{Wrong models.} Every wrong model is a modification of the forecast model only; the truth is the
2D KS equation $u_t=-\nabla^2u-\nabla^4u-\tfrac12|\nabla u|^2$ throughout, except in the hidden-field cases.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Coefficient:} $u_t=-a\nabla^2u-\nabla^4u-\tfrac12|\nabla u|^2$, $a=1.1$ or $1.25$; the most unstable
  wavenumber moves from $1/\sqrt2$ to $\sqrt{a/2}$.
\item \emph{Advection:} the KS right-hand side plus $-c\,\partial_xu$, $c=1$ or $2$, applied exactly as a Fourier
  phase shift after every step.
\item \emph{Forcing:} the KS right-hand side plus a fixed field $f(x,y)$: white noise smoothed with a
  Gaussian kernel of correlation length $5$, zero mean, scaled to standard deviation $0.2$ or $0.4$ (one
  realization, the same in every run).
\item \emph{Resolution:} the KS equation on a $64^2$ or $32^2$ grid; the initial ensemble and the analyses
  are spectrally truncated to that grid, the observations are taken at the same physical points, and
  the error is measured after zero-padding back to $128^2$.
\item \emph{Damping order:} $u_t=-a\nabla^2u-b(-\nabla^2)^{p/2}u-\tfrac g2|\nabla u|^2$ with $p=5$, $6$ or $8$. For each
  $p$, $(a,b,g)$ start from the values that minimize the one-cycle forecast error on an independent
  trajectory (40 starts, Nelder--Mead): $(0.640, 0.634, 0.996)$, $(0.436, 0.426, 0.980)$ and
  $(0.210, 0.203, 0.938)$; no $(a,b,g)$ gives the KS growth rate $K^2-K^4$ at all wavenumbers. The
  remaining one-cycle errors, $0.072$, $0.129$ and $0.210$, are the irreducible errors of the three forms.
\item \emph{Hidden field:} the truth is $u_t=\mathrm{KS}(u)+\varepsilon v$, $v_t=-v/\tau+\kappa\nabla^2u$ with $\tau=2$,
  $\kappa=-1$ and $\varepsilon=0.1$ or $0.2$, integrated with ETDRK4 on the pair; $v$ is never observed and never
  seen by the learned model, which is trained on $u$ alone. The physics-based filter's model is KS
  without $v$. The reference filter with the true equations carries its own $v$, started at zero and
  updated through its ensemble covariance with the observed $u$.
\end{itemize}

\para{Remedies.} \emph{Tuning:} RTPS $\alpha\in\{0,0.3,0.5,0.6,0.7,0.8,0.9,1.0\}$ and
$c\in\{8.25,16,24\}$ (and $4$ for advection), and multiplicative inflation $1.1$ and $1.2$ without RTPS.
\emph{Online parameter estimation:} each member carries its own value of the parameter that is wrong
(the coefficient $a$, the speed $c$, or $(a,b,g)$ jointly), started from the wrong or best-fit value
with a spread of $0.05$ ($0.3$ for the speed; $5\%$ of each value for $(a,b,g)$), and is forecast with it.
The parameters are updated with a global ensemble transform using all observations,
$\theta_a=\bar\theta+W^\top(\theta-\bar\theta)$ with the transform $W$ of the unlocalized filter computed from
the prior observation perturbations, so that the analysis mean equals the ensemble Kalman regression
of the parameters on the innovations \citep{anderson2001,ruiz2013}; the parameter spread is relaxed to
its prior with weight $0.9$ and kept above $0.005$ ($0.5\%$ for $(a,b,g)$). \emph{Bias correction and
additive model error:} on an independent trajectory, the one-cycle residuals
$r=u(t+T)-M\big(u(t)\big)$ of the wrong model $M$ from 100 true states give the bias $\bar r$, added to
every forecast, and the spectrum and standard deviation of $r-\bar r$, from which additive noise with
the same spatial spectrum is drawn and scaled by $q\in\{0,0.5,1,2\}$ \citep{dee2005}. The state-dependent
one-cycle error of Table~\ref{tab:da_model_error} is the standard deviation of $r-\bar r$.

\subsection{Cost of the observation Jacobian}
\label{app:da_jacobian}

Figure~\ref{fig:jacobian_timing} times the latent observation Jacobian at
every deployment size, with the observation network of the runs. The
coloured construction, $c=6$ forward-mode passes through the decoder,
gives the same matrix as dense reverse-mode differentiation to the last
digit (largest difference $0$ up to $132^2$), and its time grows slightly
more slowly than the latent dimension: $1.3$~ms at $44^2$ and $49$~ms at $440^2$.
Reverse mode needs one pass per observation and grows almost with the
square of the dimension, $52$ times slower at $132^2$; we did not run it
beyond that size.

\subsection{One-dimensional experiments}
\label{app:da_1d}

Table~\ref{tab:da_1d} gives the 1D results summarized in Sec.~\ref{sec:da_1d}. They use the 1D
model of Part~I, a single tiling, and either the particle flow filter or the stochastic ensemble
Kalman filter with Gaspari--Cohn localization of half-width 3 sites, without RTPS.

\para{Observation density.} With the particle flow filter at $L=200$ the skill is $5.2$, $2.9$, $1.2$ and
$1.0$ at observation spacings $1.6$, $3.1$, $6.25$ and $12.5$: with a single tiling, assimilation fails once
the spacing reaches the site length ($6.25$), well below the pattern wavelength $2\sqrt2\,\pi\approx8.9$.
An earlier version of the 1D experiment scaled the observation stride with the domain, which doubled
the spacing with every doubling of $L$; at $L=200$ its localized skill was about $1.3$, against $2.9$ at the
fixed spacing used here.

\para{Sampling error correction.} At the training size $L=100$, in an earlier comparison with 40
cycles, SEC applied to a global latent vector beat Gaspari--Cohn applied to the local field at every
ensemble size, with errors $5$--$25\%$ lower. That comparison confounds the localization with the
architecture (two different autoencoders), and at $L=100$ the taper is non-zero on 11 of the 16 sites
of the ring, so it hardly localizes. At $L=200$ the comparison is made on one latent. The
SEC-corrected covariance formed elementwise is indefinite at five of six ensemble sizes, and the
particle flow filter, which factorizes it, fails; this is a property of the matrix form, not of SEC,
and the projected and serial forms of Appendix~\ref{app:da_filters} run at every size.

\subsection{Noisy training: the round-trip test}
\label{app:da_roundtrip}

Forty true states of an independent 2D KS trajectory at $44^2$, standardized with each model's training
statistics, are perturbed and passed through the encoder and decoder; the ratio of the error after the
round trip to the error before it is below one when the round trip moves the field towards the truth.
For white grid-point noise of standard deviation $0.05$, $0.1$, $0.2$ and $0.4$ the ratio is $0.95$, $0.53$, $0.35$
and $0.29$ for the clean-trained model and $0.81$, $0.44$, $0.29$ and $0.23$ for the noisy-trained one; for
smooth noise (correlation length 4 grid points) it is $1.35$, $1.09$, $1.02$ and $0.99$ against $1.26$, $1.07$,
$1.01$ and $0.99$; for latent-space noise of standard deviation $0.05$--$0.2$ it is $1.00$--$1.05$ for both. The
reconstruction error of unperturbed states is $0.046$ and $0.039$ (\texttt{exp\_j18b\_roundtrip.py}).

\subsection{Run-to-run spread and transients}
\label{app:da_tuning}

Seeds change the initial ensemble, the observation noise and the model noise; seeds 1 and 2 also
change the truth's initial state. At $44^2$ the three seeds give $0.302$--$0.324$, and second seeds at $220^2$
and $330^2$ are within $0.01$ of the first (Table~\ref{tab:da_2d_sizes}); differences below about $0.02$ are
within run-to-run variation. Started from the encoded truth, the analysis error rises over the first
cycles as the ensemble spreads, and is within $10\%$ of its steady value from cycle 4 at every size.
Its 10-cycle means over cycles 51--100 vary by up to $\pm6\%$ at $44^2$, $\pm2\%$ at $88^2$ and $\pm1\%$ or less at $220^2$ and
beyond, the smaller domains averaging over fewer independent regions. Long runs with sparse
observations are not bitwise reproducible from run to run (multithreaded floating-point reductions,
amplified by chaos), so differences between methods smaller than the seed spread are not
significant there. The per-cycle values of every run are stored with its summary.
