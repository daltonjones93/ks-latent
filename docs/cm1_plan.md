## L. Lyapunov spectrum of the latent model as an estimate of CM1's (added 2026-11-06)
Can the trained propagator stand in for CM1's missing tangent model? Paper I's `ks_latent/analysis/lyapunov.py` takes any lattice model: `benettin` only needs a step that advances a state and a batch of tangent vectors (`torch.func.jvp` through the CM1 propagator). The latent state is 200 sites × 32 channels = 6 400 dimensions (~6 200 without the held boundary sites), so the full spectrum is feasible (float64 QR on CPU, minutes).

Caveats to state up front:
- **No attractor:** a squall line evolves over 6 h with open boundaries, so these are *finite-time* exponents over a window (e.g. the mature stage, 1–3 h), per minute, and they change as the storm evolves.
- **The latent manifold only:** scales below the 2 km sites, and CM1's acoustic and fast gravity modes, are not represented. The estimate is of the leading (convective-scale) part: λ1, the number of positive exponents, the Kaplan–Yorke dimension of the resolved dynamics, not the full CM1 spectrum.
- **No CM1 tangent model to compare with:** the ground truth comes from CM1 twin runs (L2).

## SM. Smoothing: sharper forecasts without losing accuracy (added 2026-11-06)
The surrogate smooths small-scale convection (section figures), and L1 finds it far less chaotic than CM1 (latent λ1 ~0.002–0.02 /min against CM1's ~2–3 min doubling during initiation). Much of this is the MSE objective, not capacity: beyond the predictability time of the small cells, the MSE-optimal forecast is their conditional mean, which is smooth, and a sharper deterministic field is penalized twice. So a bigger model or a transformer trained with MSE converges to the same smooth answer at long leads. The way out is two products: a smooth, accurate best estimate (MSE) and sharp, realistic samples whose average is still accurate (probabilistic training). Targets: sharp samples with the deterministic model's mean accuracy, realistic spectra and extremes, and error growth matching CM1 (L2).

## P. What the latent field knows: physical interpretation and prediction (added 2026-11-06, with P. J. van Leeuwen)
The question: the latent field is a learned, local state descriptor (32 numbers per 2 km site, 1-min dynamics). Do physical events and regimes (updraft cores, rain formation, snow and graupel aloft, the cold pool) correspond to recognizable configurations of the latent field, can the latent state and its motion predict them (when does rain begin at a site?), and which aspects of the geometry carry that information? And can storm-scale behaviour be read from the local field, or does that need a unified (domain-wide) latent space?

Principles for every test here:
- **Baselines, or the claim is empty.** The latent encodes the current state, which already contains cloud water and updrafts, so "the latent predicts rain" is trivial on its own. Every probe is compared with the same probe on (i) the physical columns themselves (the 8 × 434 values of the site) and (ii) a PCA of those columns to the same 32 dimensions; prediction also with persistence and with the surrogate's own forecast (roll, decode, threshold).
- **Gauge.** Latent coordinates are defined only up to an invertible map (an independently trained autoencoder gives different coordinates for the same physics; Phase 12 §14.1). Predictive skill and information content are coordinate-free; distances, directions and clusters are not. Every geometric claim must survive in a second autoencoder (another seed, or SM1's 64-channel model), after CCA / Procrustes alignment.
- **Context grows with lead time.** Information travels ~2 km/min (data-only reach 8–12 km per 5 min), so predicting a site's future τ min ahead needs a neighbourhood of ±2τ km: probes use a window of sites that widens with the lead, like the propagator's stencil.
- **Targets from raw CM1 output, not from the model's variables.** The raw output has the species separately (qc, qr, qi, qs, qg), surface precipitation rate (`prate`), accumulated rain, reflectivity, and surface rain / snow / graupel; the model only sees liquid and ice totals. So rain-vs-cloud and snow-vs-graupel questions need no model change at first, only targets read from the raw files (`io.transform(split=True)` already reads the species). D15 (species as model variables) only if P1 shows they are not separable from the current latent.

## Q. What the propagator alone says about CM1's dynamics (added 2026-11-06)
Suppose we only had the propagator: a translation-invariant local update rule (a learned stencil equation) on 2 km sites with a 1-min step. Its weights and latent directions are not meaningful on their own (the coordinates are defined only up to an invertible map), but its **dynamical invariants** are: eigenvalues of the linearized dynamics, growth rates, frequencies, Lyapunov exponents, periods of periodic solutions. Speeds come out in physical units because the sites have physical positions. These are directly comparable with CM1.
**Hypothesis:** the MSE-trained propagator gets the organized, deterministic dynamics right (wave speeds, the squall line's propagation and structure, the cell-regeneration period, response kernels) and the small-scale chaos wrong (L1/L2: it is far less chaotic than CM1). Either outcome says precisely what a learned surrogate knows about the true model.
Caveat: the columns are perturbations from the far-field initial column, so the quiescent latent state is (nearly) the same in every environment; the propagator sees the environment only through the storm's own structure (environment conditioning was neutral, D11). Environment dependence therefore shows up in Q2–Q3 (storm solutions), not in Q1.

## FP. A field latent whose law can be read: transport + sparse sources (added 2026-11-07, user direction)
Goal (user, 2026-11-07): a latent space from which a law useful for predicting physical phenomena can be learned and read, ideally a PDE; DA and transfer are not constraints. Design discussion in this conversation; the user's direction: latent variables are learned nonlinear combinations of the observed variables, in much lower dimension, no patches, not necessarily per-column channels.

Why KS gave no readable latent PDE, and what is different here:
- KS has one field, so its only compression was spatial coarse-graining, which is not closed: the ~50 robust sparse terms of the local closure ran through learned hidden channels (RESULTS.md, 2026-09-25).
- Its latent PDEs were trained by rollout MSE, which learns the conditional mean; the conditional mean of chaos is damped, and the latent PDE propagators collapsed to D_KY = 0 (sine_transform_pde_plan.md §11–14), the same damping SM/SM5/Q1 found here.
- CM1's variables are redundant: pressure is diagnostic, u and w share a streamfunction, and saturation adjustment collapses theta, q_v and cloud water to two conserved variables (theta_il, q_t), whose laws are transport plus a few sources (fall-out, mixing); the vorticity law is D eta/Dt = -dB/dx. So compress the variables, not space; keep x-z as the latent's coordinates; build transport in; fit the law to short-time tendencies, not long rollouts.

Architecture (target of FP2): m latent fields phi(x, z) on the x-z plane (no patches; height a real coordinate), encoder nonlocal (2D convolutions), decoder a local function of phi's 2-jet (lets few fields carry several variables, e.g. u, w from one streamfunction), law d phi/dt + v(phi) . grad phi = S(phi, grad phi, grad^2 phi) + noise, S a sparse library (polynomial, threshold, relaxation terms) plus a small penalized residual, trained jointly with the autoencoder on reconstruction + short-time tendency consistency + sparsity; rollout only as a light stability term.
